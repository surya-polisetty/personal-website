I’m the co-founder and CTO of Mindlabs. We build the hardware, firmware, cloud and AI systems that let companies see and act on what is happening across their physical operations, in real time. Since we started, that has become more than 100,000 IoT devices in the field and a business past $2M in revenue.

I’m responsible for the technology end to end — circuit boards, embedded firmware, the distributed backend, and the AI layer on top of it, which is where most of my attention goes now — and for a good deal of what surrounds it: the engineering team, enterprise deployments, where and how we manufacture, what we charge. Very little of that came with the title. There isn’t a layer on that list I haven’t been deep inside, and not one I hold alone today.

The habit underneath all of that is starting beneath a problem rather than inside it — understanding why a thing is built the way it is before deciding what to do about it. It predates the job: two years of independent research in mathematical logic and physics, before I came back to industry. Building was never the point on its own; it is what tends to follow once you understand something well enough to see a better version of it.

What I have optimised for since is capability rather than comfort or title — being able to take on problems the earlier version of me could not have. If I can learn a thing, build it, or lead it, I have rarely found a good reason not to.

Some things I’ve built

A platform at fleet scale. Device firmware, provisioning, over-the-air updates and a distributed backend carrying real-time telemetry from more than 100,000 deployed devices.

AI in production. Leading AI integration across that platform, turning operational data into decisions rather than dashboards. Separately, I’ve built agent systems directly on top of LLM APIs — planning, tool execution, memory, reasoning loops — rather than assembling them out of frameworks.

An engineering organisation. Hired and led multidisciplinary teams across hardware, embedded, cloud, web, mobile and AI — deliberately built so that they run without me in the room.

The commercial side. Enterprise relationships from first conversation through deployment and scale, pricing, and the unit economics underneath it.

Hardware into production. Manufacturing set up from scratch in China — vendor sourcing through to volume.
